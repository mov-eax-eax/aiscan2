# Asterion-9 — an alienologist’s working notes

**Site:** manufactured island, cloud deck, Asterion-9 (K-type star, 0.82 g, 1.31 bar,
19% O₂, 31-hour day, permanent civil twilight).
**Programme:** a neutral-ground entertainment park and embassy for six biospheres.
**Rule:** question the object before drawing it. The park is not a human carnival with
alien paint; it is what six different body plans agree to call a good time.

## 1. Scale is the first design decision

One scene unit is one metre. Without a stated scale, detail is meaningless.

| thing | size | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| island | 310 m across | needs its own weather and a visible horizon |
| bloom crown | 162 m tall | visible from the next island; a landmark before it is a building |
| embassy pavilions | 28–52 m | doors must be 4–7 m; crowd bodies 1.5–1.8 m |
| bloom stem | 96 m | a lift, not a stair; three service galleries |
| halo wheel | 26 m diameter | 13 m radius; ten pods; 8-minute cycle |
| drop tower | 33 m | deliberately too tall for comfort |
| carousel | 16 m diameter | eight body-morphing seats |
| skyway | 48–53 m radius | one full loop every 20 minutes |
| thal grazers | 6–11 m | large enough to be weather, not scenery |
| maintenance drones | 0.7 m | small enough to be ignored until they swarm |
| the Quiet Object | 18 m | tall enough to make the promenade detour around it |

## 2. What does entertainment mean here?

Not a human midway. The six biospheres do not share a thrill response.

- **Veyra** (thin, high-gravity lineage) reads altitude as intimacy. The halo wheel is a
  social instrument: pods are private, the rotation is slow, the view is the ride.
- **Keth** (crystalline, resonance-sensing) has no eyes in the human sense. The
  resonance stage projects sound into visible interference patterns. The audience
  watches with its skin.
- **Ilo** (amphibious, tidal) needs water and humidity. The carousel sits in a mist
  basin; the seats curve to a body that has no fixed joints.
- **Sable** (mechanical, inherited industry) finds safety in over-engineered machinery.
  The drop tower is a ritual of controlled failure; the maintenance drones are its
  ushers.
- **Morrow** (colonial, many-bodied) does not ride alone. The skyway cars are booked by
  the group, not the seat.
- **Orion** (unknown) does not queueline. The Quiet Object may not be an attraction at
  all; the park keeps it because removing it would be a bigger claim than leaving it.

## 3. The ecosystem is the park

The park is not a zoo with rides bolted on. It is a habitat that has been taught to
entertain.

- **Thal grazers** crop the terrace moss. They are not exhibits; they are employees with
  right of way. Their herds produce the park’s low-frequency sound bed.
- **Maintenance drones** are descended from industrial cleaners. They are mechanical,
  but they have learned to perform: the nightly light show is partly their flight paths.
- **Sky mantas** filter the upper atmosphere. They only descend when the carousel is
  running, for reasons the park does not publish.
- **Fungal mats** under the stone produce the mist. The mist is respiration, not weather.
- **Lantern flora** are pollinated by drones. The light is a by-product, not a decoration.

## 4. Materials are a taxonomy

- **Grown stone** — terraces, cultured around a seed lattice. It repairs slowly.
- **Living glass** — pavilion domes, still warm, slightly uneven.
- **Cable muscle** — skyway and bloom veins; tensioned, not rigid.
- **Embassy alloy** — the mechanical quarter; deliberately over-engineered.
- **Null matter** — the Quiet Object. It returns light in colours that were not in the
  source. It has no shadow that matches the sun.

## 5. Questions the park refuses to answer

1. Who built the bloom, and why does it keep its core hot after the parks close?
2. Why does the Quiet Object’s shadow point away from the star?
3. What do the mantas eat, and why do they arrive only when the carousel turns?
4. Why do the embassy nameplates translate differently each time they are read?
5. Is the park an embassy with rides, or a ride with embassies?
6. What does the park smell like to a species with no nose?
7. Where do the visitors go at second moonrise?

An entertainment park is allowed to leave a few things unexplained. That is where the
audience lives.
