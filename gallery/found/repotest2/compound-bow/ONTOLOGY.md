# Compound bow ontology

A formal description of a modern compound bow: what its parts are, how they connect, and the
cam-and-cable mechanics that make it compound. It is written to drive a parametric 3D model
- **geometry is authored from this data, physics is a readout** - and it is checked by
`tools/validate.mjs`.

This file is generated by reading `ontology/compound-bow.ontology.json`. Edit the JSON, then
regenerate with `node tools/render-ontology.mjs`; do not hand-edit this document.

## 1. Why an ontology first

A compound bow is not one mechanism; it is five that only make sense together: a rigid riser,
elastic limbs, non-circular eccentrics, a cable train and the accessory stack. Getting the
*names* and *interfaces* right before modelling anything means the model can be assembled,
exploded, measured and verified part by part, and means the physics has an unambiguous object
to attach to. The ontology separates four things that are easy to confuse: the **class** of a
part, the **individual** fitted to this bow, the **material** it is made from, and the
**relations** that hold between parts.

## 7. Mechanics

The limbs store the energy; the eccentrics and cables decide how hard and how long you must pull to load them, and how little you must hold at full draw. The bow is drawn by cables pulling the limb tips TOWARD EACH OTHER, not by the string bending the limbs backward.

### 7.1 Eccentric topologies

| topology | cams | idlers | cables | definition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Single cam + idler** (`singleCam`) | 1 | 1 | 1 | One driven cam (usually lower) and one round idler wheel. The string runs from the cam over the idler; a buss cable ties the cam to the opposite limb. |
| **Hybrid cam** (`hybridCam`) | 2 | - | 1 | Two eccentrics controlled by one cable; the second cam is often a control cam rather than a mirrored drive cam. |
| **Dual cam** (`dualCam`) | 2 | - | 2 | Two mirrored cams, each with its own cable anchored to the limb or cam. |
| **Binary cam** (`binaryCam`) | 2 | - | 2 | Two mirrored cams; each cable is anchored to the OPPOSITE cam, so the pair is self-correcting and stays synchronised. No cable is anchored to a limb. |
| **Quad cam** (`quadCam`) | 2 | - | 2 | Two cams with multiple tracks; mechanically a binary variant with additional take-up. |
| **Hinged / limb-mounted eccentric** (`hinged`) | 2 | - | - | Eccentric carried on a hinged link rather than directly on the limb tip; uncommon. |

### 7.2 The reference cam system

**Crosscentric cam with Advanced Vectoring System and SwitchWeight-X modules** (topology: `binaryCam`).

* The cam is non-circular: its effective radius (the moment arm from the axle to the string/cable contact) changes as it rotates, so a roughly constant cable tension produces a shaped draw-force curve.
* The AVS uses two small discs per cam mounted OFFSET to the axle and anchored to the ends of the harness cable. As they rotate non-concentrically they shift the force vector, storing more energy at the front of the draw and increasing let-off at the back.
* The SwitchWeight module is the interface between the module and the cam tracks; swapping it changes draw length, let-off (80 or 85%) and peak weight in steps without changing limbs.
* The draw stop on each cam strikes the limb at full draw, giving a hard back wall and fixing the anchor; its position also trims draw length.

### 7.3 Draw cycle (210 degrees of cam rotation)

*Cam rotation and lever-arm change are read from the reference mechanism photograph (commons_cam_forcedraw.jpg): five poses at 0, 45, 90, 135 and ~210 degrees of rotation, annotated with lever arms and the cable take-up length.*

| phase | draw | cam rotation | force | behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **brace** | 0 | 0 | brace preload (small, set by limb tiller) | String at rest at the brace height; cables pre-tensioned; limbs already bent from their moulded shape. |
| **frontEnd** | 0-10 | 0-45 | rises steeply to the peak within the first third | Cams turn and their long moment arms load the limbs hard; this is where a compound stores most of its energy per inch. |
| **plateau** | 10-22 | 45-135 | roughly flat near the peak ('stacking' if it rises, 'soft' if it falls) | The effective radius stays high; the archer does most of the work but feels an even pull. |
| **letOff** | 22-DL | 135-200 | falls sharply toward the holding weight | The moment arm collapses as the string approaches the axle; stored energy is nearly maximum while the force needed falls. |
| **wall** | DL | 210 | the holding weight | Draw stop contacts the limb; a hard, repeatable stop. The archer stops pulling. |
| **release** | DL->0 | 210->0 | limb torque drives the cams back | The cables unwind the cams; the limbs pull the string forward and launch the arrow. |

### 7.4 Let-off and energy

`letOff = (peakDrawWeight - holdingWeight) / peakDrawWeight x 100.`

| peak (lb) | let-off (%) | holding (lb) |
|---|---|---|
| 70 | 80 | 14 |
| 70 | 85 | 10.5 |
| 80 | 85 | 12 |

* Stored energy: `E_stored = integral F(s) ds over the power stroke` - the area under the force-draw curve.
* Arrow energy: `KE = 0.5 * m * v^2 / gc, gc = 32.174 lb.ft/(lbf.s^2)`.
* Efficiency: 70-85 percent.
* IBO sanity check: 350 gr at 343 fps gives 91.4 ft-lb, implying 114.3 ft-lb stored at 80 percent efficiency - a stored/peak ratio of 1.63, **above** the 1.0-1.4 class band, so the IBO speed must not be read as a guaranteed delivered speed.

### 7.5 Synchronisation

Both cams must reach the wall together and stay in phase through the draw; if they drift, the nock travels off-axis and accuracy suffers.

* Mechanisms: cable lengths (harness/buss); timing marks on cam and limb; draw-stop length; cam-to-cam cable anchors on binary cams self-correct.
* Observable: nock travel: the path of the nock relative to the arrow pass..

## 8. Operating states

* **Brace / at rest** - String at brace height; limbs pre-bent by cable tension; no draw force on the string beyond preload.
* **Drawing** - String pulled back; cams rotate and cables load the limbs; force rises along the F-d curve.
* **Peak weight** - Maximum force early in the draw.
* **Let-off / valley** - Force falls as the effective moment arm collapses.
* **Wall / full draw** - Draw stops bear on the limbs; holding weight is 15-20% of peak; the archer anchors.
* **Release** - Release aid fires; cables unwind; limbs drive the string forward.
* **Recovery** - String is arrested by the string stop; dampers and string silencers dissipate vibration; cams return to brace.

## 9. Build order

The assembly order the eventual model should explode along.

1. **Riser platform** - The rigid frame every later part references. (11 parts)
2. **Limb pockets and limbs** - Limbs pivot in the pockets and are preloaded by the tiller bolts. (12 parts)
3. **Axles, bearings, cams, modules, AVS, draw stops** - Cams go in the limb slots on bearings; modules and draw stops set draw length and wall. (16 parts)
4. **String and cables** - Requires a bow press: limbs must be compressed to relieve cable tension. (6 parts)
5. **Timing and tuning** - Set cam timing, draw length, let-off and tiller; verify with timing marks. (2 parts)
6. **Dampers, string furniture, accessories** - Everything the archer touches, plus the projectile. (19 parts)

## 10. Validating and extending

    node tools/validate.mjs

Checks referential integrity (every parent, class, material and part reference resolves), that
the class tree is acyclic, that every part is grouped into a subsystem and a build phase, the
mechanics consistency, and - most usefully - that every derived dimension recomputes from its
own formula.

To extend: add a class, then an individual of it, then place that individual in a subsystem and
a build phase, and re-run the validator. It reports what is missing.
