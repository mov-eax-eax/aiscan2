# Continual Learning for LLMs and Agents — China, 2025-01 → 2026-10

Scope: continual pretraining / continual instruction tuning, catastrophic forgetting in LLMs, agent memory and lifelong agents, model editing and unlearning as CL, continual RLHF/RLVR, and CL benchmarks for LLMs. 14 records in `llm.json`, every one verified against arXiv abs + DOI/Crossref/OpenAlex. Scan base: OpenAlex returned 2,151 CN-affiliated "continual learning" works since 2025-01-01 (1,577 in Computer Science) and 1,454 CN works with "continual" in the title since 2025-01-01; that pool — not a hand-picked list — is what the bullets below summarise.

- **The field has moved from replay to PEFT-plus-merging, and the 2026 Chinese output is almost entirely replay-free.** Only 2 of the 14 records use replay at all, and neither replays stored data (Continual Prompt Replay regenerates old responses with the current policy; ReCAP retrieves an external knowledge base). The economic driver is explicit in the papers: constant historical footprint and no data retention (ChainLoRA), or adapter budgets cut 50–75% (Monarch-MoE).

- **The forgetting-mechanism debate has shifted from "how much do we forget" to "which term in the update causes it".** Three competing mechanistic accounts coexist in this window: (i) *representational superposition* — CAS Institute of Automation derives the interference term of the lifelong-editing closed form and shows it vanishes only when knowledge is not superposed (AAAI 2025); (ii) *finite-precision leakage* — PaLoRA shows that even under exact nullspace projection, updates leak into the history subspace along multiple directions, and the leak scales with the effective rank of accumulated knowledge; (iii) *supervision budget* — TSRL bounds forgetting by bounding the cumulative parameter update, so forgetting is a function of how much supervised signal enters, not of geometry alone.

- **The nullspace/orthogonality consensus of 2024 is now being attacked from inside the lineage.** PaLoRA's pacing law `s* = sqrt(R/c)` directly indicts the field's standard recipe (O-LoRA-style projection + a small fixed learning rate) as a heuristic that only slows an unavoidable accumulation; GORP argues the low-rank constraint itself is the bottleneck and escapes it by updating full-rank and low-rank parameters inside one shared low-rank gradient subspace (ACL 2025). The trajectory is: constrain the update → constrain it better → prove the constraint leaks.

- **Scale does not buy stability — the opposite.** The most-cited record here (132 citations, IEEE TASLP 2025) shows forgetting during continual instruction tuning is universal from 1B to 7B and *worsens* with scale, replicated on Qwen2.5-Inst 3B→14B, and that decoder-only models forget less than encoder-decoder ones. This is the empirical premise the entire Chinese PEFT-CL literature is built to refute, and it is why almost no paper in this window proposes plain sequential fine-tuning.

- **Merging is being re-derived as geometry, not averaging.** ChainLoRA separates directional overlap from coefficient coupling between task vectors, keeps regularisation cost constant by orthogonalising only against the last carrier, and does the merge with Procrustes-aligned adaptive SVD (SJTU/HUST). This is the clearest statement of where the sub-field is heading: the merge step becomes a constrained geometric operation with a theoretical bound on inter-task interference.

- **Architecture is re-entering through structured experts.** Monarch-MoE replaces dense LoRA factors with block-diagonal factors plus a fixed permutation, which gives *blockwise gradient isolation* — an update to one block-pair can only move one output block — with first-task forgetting of 0.52 pp (MLLM-DCL) and 0.10 pp (UCIT) at ~50–75% fewer adapter parameters. Interference control is being pushed inside the adapter rather than enforced by a projection outside it.

- **Benchmarks for LLM CL are migrating from QA accuracy to agency.** The two benchmark records are both about agents: EmbodiedCLBench (PKU/BAAI) evaluates whether an embodied agent can recombine skills from basic tasks to solve harder unseen tasks, and finds that experience helps consistently but *does not scale with the number of learning samples* — current agents cannot extract more from more experience. Continual Reasoning Gym (PKU/BAAI/BIT) does the same for RLVR with five text/visual task sequences. Both papers report that the dominant failure is not forgetting but failure to reuse.

- **A third camp — don't update the weights at all — is now credible and cheap.** CMT (HIT, AAAI 2025) freezes every parameter and compresses new documents into a retrievable memory bank (+4.07 EM / +4.19 F1 on StreamingQA with Llama-2-7B), while the agent-memory work (CMI) shows the same stability–plasticity trade-off reappears in natural-language memory: benign accumulation is harmless, but relationship-specific interference suppresses update plasticity with little stability gain, and poisoned memories respond more to *authority* cues than to recency.

- **Continual alignment is the newest and least settled sub-area, and it reframes what "forgetting" even measures.** "Beyond Forgetting" (continual RLVR) decomposes the gap to multitask RLVR and shows forgetting explains only part of it — sequential training forgets modestly yet still loses, because it also fails to exploit *shared reasoning* across tasks; only prompt replay reaches multitask parity. TSRL attacks the same problem from the supervision side and reports +9.6 points of retained prior-task capability over SFT→RL pipelines on ALFWorld/WebShop/Deep Search at 7B and 32B.

- **Division of labour across Chinese labs is stable and legible.** CAS Institute of Automation owns editing theory and agent RL post-training (Knowledge in Superposition; TSRL); CAS ICT owns structured-expert architecture (Monarch-MoE); Tsinghua/PKU/NJU own the LoRA-CL core (GainLoRA at NeurIPS 2025, PaLoRA, ChainLoRA); HIT and Westlake+Tencent own the memory/frozen-model and empirical-forgetting poles; BAAI co-authors the agent benchmarks; Huawei and Tencent appear as the applied collaborators (ReCAP; the TASLP study). Every record has a mainland-China first author.

## Caveats

- **Citation counts are a snapshot from OpenAlex at scan time (2026-10-17) and are near-zero for the 2026 arXiv records** — nine of the 14 are preprint-stage with 0 citations, so "impact" here means venue + mechanism novelty, not measured uptake.
- **The 2026 records are arXiv-only; no ICLR/ICML 2026 proceedings are indexed yet for these.** Venue fields for them say "arXiv preprint" deliberately rather than projecting an acceptance.
- **One record is a 2023-preprint / 2025-journal landmark** (Luo et al., IEEE TASLP 2025; arXiv v5 revised 2025-01-05). It is retained as the field's reference empirical baseline, not as new work.
- **Thin coverage of continual *pre-training* proper.** Chinese CPT papers in this window are overwhelmingly domain CPT (power grid, Tibetan, e-commerce, codec speech) rather than CL methodology; the closest methodological record is EoupCT (Jilin University), which protects *pre-trained* general knowledge during continual fine-tuning rather than running a CPT stream. Excluded surveys: SCUT's ACM CSUR 2025 survey and SCUT/Tencent's TPAMI lifelong-agent roadmap — useful context, but not primary contributions.
- **Deliberately excluded:** single-co-author China links (e.g. a CUHK-Shenzhen last author on a US-first-authored agent-skill paper), pure vision class-incremental work that dominates the raw OpenAlex count, and national-security-adjacent domain CL (intrusion detection, malware) with no LLM methodology.
