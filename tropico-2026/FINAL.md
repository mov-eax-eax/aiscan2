# TROPICO 2026 — the catalogue

*República de Trópico, 2026. Seven vector paintings, one country, one year.*

> "El sol es la deuda." — The sun is the debt.

## What this is

The collection began with a direction — *Spanish realism, the tropics in 2026* — and was then
handed to three artists and a curator with one instruction: do not stay in that language. Each
following work re-looks at the same country through an entirely different visual system. What
holds the set together is not style but inventory and weather: mango, machete, tinaco, solar
panel, satellite dish, monobloc chair, palm, cable, sea wall, and a sun that is both the national
resource and the national debt.

## How the set was made

**Round A — three languages.** `01` set the anchor in Spanish realism: a bodegón as the
inventory of a household economy, one shutter of light, the glass of water as the brightest
event. `02` moved the same country into Sorolla's noon: high key, violet shadows, fisherfolk
with their backs to us. `03` went flat: a decaying Ministry of the Future as a tropical-
modernist poster, a brise-soleil built as a module plus permutation, one coral portal breaking
the rhythm.

**The critique.** The curator scored all three below the ship line and refused the obvious
diagnoses. `01` was not a material problem but a silhouette problem — texture on a circle is
still a circle. `02` had one fatal compositing fault and a flat shade. `03` did not lack weight,
it lacked light. And, as a set, they were three exercises rather than one country: a repeating
skeleton, drifting accents, and no painting in which the sun actually hurt.

**Round B — three more languages.** `04` took the collection into tenebrism: a customs officer
after the power cut, paper as the brightest event. `05` became the state's eye: a thermal frame
at 03:00 whose real subject is what the sensor cannot see (glass is opaque to long-wave IR).
`06` turned the country into botany: a plate for a plant that does not exist, whose leaves are
solar panels and whose seed is a cut cable.

**Fix passes.** Each round-A piece went back for one targeted pass on the fault the critic named
by place, not for a repaint. `03` cleared the line; `02` climbed; `01` documented its failure
honestly rather than pretending.

**Synthesis.** `07` closes the set as a three-ink screenprint retablo: the private room, the
threshold onto blinding sea light, the state's thermal eye, and the inventory printed beneath
like a register of saints.

## What the iteration taught

1. **A style is a decision about light, not a palette.** The pieces that work are the ones where
   one light source does the moral work; the ones that don't are the ones where colour is doing
   the job instead.
2. **The medium fights back.** SVG does geometry, silhouettes, flat plates and pattern
   beautifully, and plastic spheres and cloth badly. Every piece that succeeded moved *towards*
   what the medium does well.
3. **Texture cannot save a wrong contour.** Confirmed on `01` twice, by two different artists.
4. **The figure is still the unsolved problem.** Four works contain a person; in every one the
   person is the weakest form. The country is convincing; its people are still signs.
5. **Critique is a production tool.** The single biggest jumps in the set came from a named
   fault in a named place, not from another pass of general polish.

## Honest state

This is a strong student collection, not a museum one. Its best works (`06`, `07`, `03`) are
achieved within their languages; its weakest (`04`, `01`) are held together by one good decision
each. Every SVG is hand-authored, self-contained and editable; every render is exactly what the
source produces.
