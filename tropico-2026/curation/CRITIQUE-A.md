## 01 · EL INVENTARIO — maestro — 28/60

**What it is.** A dark room, a concrete counter, seven objects in a row, one diagonal pocket of shutter
light on the wall. Quiet, frontal, grave. The mood is right; the inventory is not yet legible.

| axis | score | the place in the image that justifies it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 composition & value | 5 | The counter edge at y≈1010 and the four value masses hold at 400 px, but the subject is a row of seven objects in the bottom quarter, and the top 45% (y 0–540, x 0–1100) is a single flat near-black — a stage, not air. |
| 2 light & atmosphere | 6 | One sun from the upper right, every shadow agrees, and the pocket's hard shutter edge from (250, 560) to the right edge at (1600, 300) is a real light event — but the claimed cast shadows on the lit wall are not legible on a single object. |
| 3 material truth | 3 | The mango (x≈590–860, y≈840–1040) is a perfect circle with two hard crescent overlays; the glass (x≈1110–1215, y≈540–1000) is a straight tube with a bright ellipse floating at mid-height; the machete (y≈1000–1060) is a bar. Only the concrete has a material. |
| 4 Trópico 2026 | 4 | The controller's display is genuinely 2026, but four of the seven canon objects are unreadable without the title, and the room could be any decade. |
| 5 craft of the medium | 5 | No seams, no clipped filters, no text — but the blur is uniform, the only hard edges are the counter and the controller's display, and the grain never touches the light. |
| 6 risk | 5 | The low light pocket is a real bet (Zurbarán's register line + Cotán's edge-separation) and it reorganised the picture; it half-landed, because the objects still read front-lit, so the silhouette idea never arrived. |

**Self-score check: 28 confirmed, diagnosis refuted.** The artist names material truth as the failure and
would spend two passes on texture. Material is the symptom. The cause is one level earlier: **silhouette**.
A textured circle is still a circle. The mango has no mango profile; the glass has no taper, no water
surface in the right place, no refraction; the fan blade reads as a white knife; the machete as a pipe.
Texture on those forms will produce a *detailed* row of generic solids. And the second claim — that the
objects' shadows fall on the lit wall and make them sit in a room — is not visible in the render: I can find
a soft dark bar at the top right and a smudge to the right of the glass, but not the jar's or the mango's
shadow shapes. The idea is in the SVG; it is not in the picture.

**Three fixes, ranked.**

1. **Draw the objects' profiles before any more material passes.** Give the mango its asymmetry (a beak at
   the lower right, two shoulders, a stem dimple) and delete the hard crescent overlays. Give the glass a
   taper, a true rim ellipse, the water surface at the top third, and delete the floating bright ellipse.
   Give the fan blade (x≈1150–1480, y≈1010–1080) the curved paddle and the ribbed root. Give the machete a
   handle, a ferrule, a taper to the point, and an edge line rather than a tube's highlight. Give the form a
   fold, a folded corner and the fold's shadow. *Acceptance: four 100% greyscale crops, each object named
   correctly by someone who has not read the brief.*
2. **Make the light transmit and land.** The glass must show the wall's cream pocket through it, displaced
   and inverted (clip an inverted copy of the pocket inside the glass), plus a caustic on the counter as a
   bright, slightly warped patch. The cast shadows must be shapes with edges that start at each object's
   contact and point left and slightly back. Replace the mango's hard cut with a soft terminator and one
   waxy micro-specular at the shoulder. *Acceptance: cover the pocket — the objects still read as lit from
   the same sun; uncover it — the brightest small event is a transmitted light, and no shadow floats.*
3. **Give the void a shape.** The top 45% is one tone. Lower the pocket's hard edge so it crosses the jar's
   shoulder and the mango's top, and put the second shutter blade high in the dark at a value 8–10% above
   the black, soft-edged, so the void has two tones and a direction. *Acceptance: posterise to four values —
   the top field contains two tones, and the pocket's edge touches at least one object.*

**Do not change.** The register line at y≈1010 and the black under it; the counter's light pool; the
controller's display (the one 2026 object that reads); the warmth of the lit concrete; the absence of text.
**One question.** Which object is the inventory *about*? Seven things share the frame equally; a ledger
needs a first entry.

## 02 · LA SIESTA DE LOS PECES — colorist — 31/60

**What it is.** A high-key beach at noon: cream sky, pale sea, three fisherfolk from behind, an awning at
the top right, a violet shade crossing the foreground. The most beautiful of the three and the most
damaged by a single fault.

| axis | score | the place in the image that justifies it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 composition & value | 6 | The awning's diagonal shade edge is the best compositional decision in round A, and the figures are spaced in depth rather than in a line — but the sky (y 0–340, left two thirds) is a dead cream field and the brightest mass in the frame is empty. |
| 2 light & atmosphere | 6 | The temperature logic is right (blazed warm white vs violet-white, warm bounce off the sand) and the awning's shadow cuts the foreground convincingly — but the sun's direction is ambiguous (the central figure's screen-left arm is lit while the shadows fall left), and there is glare on the sea but not in the air. |
| 3 material truth | 4 | The white blouse (x≈700–850, y≈540–800) is a flat sheet with a soft gradient, the sea is three flat bands, the crates are cards; cloth, skin and water share one edge law. |
| 4 Trópico 2026 | 5 | Fish, crates, net, ice, phone and a leaning solar panel read as 2026 fisherfolk — but "la siesta de los peces" does not: the catch is a small detail in a crate, and the picture is a stroll, not a rest. |
| 5 craft of the medium | 4 | The right-hand figure (x≈1040–1190, y≈650–1060) is translucent — the pavement reads through its torso and there is no head under the hat — which reads as a compositing error on the nearest figure on that side; the kiosk's mark (x≈1350–1450, y≈540–600) is an illegible grey smear; the solar panel has no contact shadow. |
| 6 risk | 6 | "Paint the shade, not the objects" is a real bet and the diagonal proves it; it half-lands because the shade interior is one flat wash. |

**Self-score check.** No number was given; the written self-critique is accurate about the flat shade and
the timid brushwork, and it misses the first problem: the ghost.

**Three fixes, ranked.**

1. **Kill the ghost.** Make the right-hand figure opaque, give it a head and neck under the hat, and if it
   is a vendor, draw the tray as an object with a shadow. *Acceptance: at 100% no background shows through
   the body; at 400 it reads as a person.*
2. **Make the shade do the work.** Rebuild the violet field (x≈1050–1600, y≈450–1100) from three bounced
   lights: warm ochre rising from the sand, sea-green at the top, violet between them; two or three
   hard-edged colour shifts, because the shade's edge is hard. On the white cloth, one edge should blow out
   and the shade side go violet-white, not grey. *Acceptance: a 100% crop of the shade shows three hues;
   the central blouse has a blown edge and a violet edge.*
3. **Give the top a job.** Put the sun into the top right as a blown patch with a hard-ish edge (not a
   gradient vignette); make the awning translucent — warm coral glow through the cloth, hem shadow, the
   pole's shadow on the cloth; and either make the kiosk's mark a deliberate worn stencil (two or three
   strokes, one broken) or delete it. *Acceptance: at 400 the top right is an event, and the eye is not
   sent out of the frame.*

**Do not change.** The diagonal shade edge; the palette (cream / violet / coral is right); the two figures
from behind; the fish on the crate; the net's mesh; the solar panel as an object.
**One question.** Where is the siesta? The title promises fish at rest in the sun and the picture gives me
people walking. Decide whether the catch is the subject — then it must win the frame — or change the title,
not the picture.

## 03 · PABELLÓN MINISTERIO DEL FUTURO — vectorista — 31/60

**What it is.** A tropical-modernist pavilion raked by one sun, a coral portal, palms cut-outs, a sea strip
and a monobloc chair. The most designed of the three and the least felt.

| axis | score | the place in the image that justifies it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 composition & value | 6 | The cropped mass, the diagonal shadow and the palms as a counterweight hold at 400 px — but the left wall (x 0–500, y 0–800) is 40% of the canvas carrying nothing but stripes, and the coral portal is too small to be the single focal event it is meant to be. |
| 2 light & atmosphere | 5 | One sun and a genuinely designed shadow (parallel on the facade, fanned on the ground) — but the shadow is only about 15–20% darker than the lit concrete, so the noon never blazes, and the sky is a flat fill with no air. |
| 3 material truth | 4 | The formwork grain runs unbroken across pilasters, recesses and the light/shadow boundary (visible at 100% on the light strips), so the building is one printed surface; the palms are single flat cut-outs; the sea is a flat strip. The chair is the exception: it reads. |
| 4 Trópico 2026 | 5 | Solar array, dish, boarded bay, cable and the monobloc chair are real systems — but the Ministry exists only in the title, and apart from the dot at the door (x≈665–700, y≈810–880) the place is empty. |
| 5 craft of the medium | 5 | No seams or clipping, but the chair's shadow is blurred while the palm shadows are hard-edged, the figure smears at the leg, and the shore's rows of identical little trees are repetition, not planting. |
| 6 risk | 6 | The pavilion cropped by the top edge plus the invented shadow (parallel on the wall, fanned on the ground) is a visible bet that could have read as a mistake, and it doesn't. |

**Self-score check: 38 is too high; I make it 31.** The left-heavy diagnosis is correct but it is not the
main cost. A 38 implies axis 2 and axis 4 at 7, which the render does not support: there is no heat, no
glare, and no legible Ministry. The piece's real costs are light (5), material (4) and the empty left wall
— all one-pass fixes, none requiring a re-block. It is closer to shipping than 01 and has more to protect.

**Three fixes, ranked.**

1. **Deepen the shadow, blow the light.** One value/palette pass. The lit concrete and the shadow are about
   20 points apart; tropical sun is 40+. Take the shadow field down to a cool green-grey around 55–60% and
   the lit faces to near-white, with the sand sitting between them, not above the shadow. This painting is
   currently 53% mid-tone by measurement; that is why it reads as a print. *Acceptance: greyscale posterised
   to four values — the shadow field is one of the two darks, the lit facade one of the two lights; at 400
   the building looks hot.*
2. **Put an event in the left half.** Break the brise-soleil once — a missing fin, a bent one, a hole — so
   one hard blade of sun lands inside the shadow field on the wall. That is the piece's argument: if the
   Ministry survives as a mark on a wall, let the mark be made of light. *Acceptance: at 400 the left half
   has a first thing that is not the stripes, and the rhythm is broken exactly twice — the portal and the
   light gap.*
3. **Material: split the formwork grain per mass, and stain.** Clip the board grain to each mass with a
   different phase so the pilaster and the recess no longer share one continuous pattern; add two or three
   directional stains (a vertical bleed from the roof edge, salt bloom at the base, a rust run from the dish
   bolt); give the palms a lit and a shaded tone so they stop being single flat cut-outs. *Acceptance: 100%
   crops show the pilaster's grain not lining up with the recess, one stain that follows gravity, and two
   tones in the palms.*

**Do not change.** The coral portal and the decision that it is the only saturated note; the monobloc chair
(the best-drawn object in round A); the cropped top edge; the palms as shapes; the sea's one strip, one
ship, one shore line.
**One question.** Where is the Ministry? The title names an institution and the picture gives me a
building. If it survives only as a stencil, I need to see the stencil — or the title is doing work the
picture has not done.

## COLLECTION COHERENCE

They read as three exercises by three hands, not yet as one country in one year. Four reasons, in order of
danger. First, **one composition skeleton is repeating**: each piece puts its subject in a band across the
lower third and leaves a large field above — 01 a black void, 02 a cream sky, 03 sky plus an empty wall.
Seven pieces built on that skeleton will read as one picture painted seven times, and the gallery will look
like a style, which is the opposite of the brief. Second, **the accent discipline is drifting**: 02 and 03
both carry coral and a saturated teal (crate, sea); the brief allows one accent, never both. Third, and
most important: **nobody has painted glare**. The collection's thesis is that the sun is the debt, and the
merciless noon is still missing — 01 is shutter-dark (correct), 02 is a beautiful haze rather than a blaze,
03's shadows are so light that the sun reads as a printing decision. At least one piece must make the sun
hurt. Fourth, **the medium is converging**: 01 and 02 are both soft-gradient vector painting and the lanes
differ by palette more than by language; if 04–06 become the same illustration in different colours, the
collection fails as a set even if each piece ships.

What does read as one place, and should be leaned on: the objects. 03's chair, 02's fish crate and 01's
charge controller are unmistakably from the same world, and none of the three people is posed for us. The
coherence is in the inventory, not in the style — which is exactly what 07's predella will depend on, since
each piece must yield one quotable shape at 160 × 160 px. Tonight, those shapes are: 01's glass and
controller, 02's awning diagonal and fish crate, 03's chair and coral portal. Protect them.

**Recommendation.** Ship nothing yet. 01 needs one drawing pass; 02 needs one bug fix plus one light pass;
03 needs one contrast pass. Each of those is a single pass and each should clear 34. I would look at all
three again after those passes, and I would not spend another pass on 01's material until its silhouettes
are right.
