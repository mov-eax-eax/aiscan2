# REFS — the reference shelf
TROPICO 2026 · curator's working file · read with RUBRIC.md

## How to use this file

A reference is not a style to copy. It is a **mechanism to borrow**: a decision someone made about
light, edge, sequence or scale that solves a problem this collection also has. Every entry is written
as **STEAL** (the mechanism, named precisely), **AVOID** (what the same maker does that would sink us),
and **LANE** (where it lands).

Rule 1: if you cannot state the mechanism in one sentence, you have not found the reference — you have
found a mood. Moods produce pastiche.

Rule 2: none of these is a "style". Velázquez is not "Spanish realism"; he is a painter of what light
does to a wet surface. Steal the wet surface, not the Spanishness.

Rule 3: every entry ends with a TEST. The test is how we check the mechanism is actually present in
the SVG and not just in the NOTES.

## A01 · Juan Sánchez Cotán — Still Life with Quince, Cabbage, Melon and Cucumber

**c.1600–02 · San Diego Museum of Art · LANE 01 (also 04)**

**See.** A shallow stone niche, black as a hole. Four vegetables hang by cords so that they descend in
a curve — the "hyperbolic parabola" — each one isolated, each one a different red or green, lit from the
upper left with a light so even that almost nothing casts a shadow. The tops of the cords are outside
the frame.

**STEAL.**
1. **Suspension as composition.** The objects are hung on a curve, not arranged on a table, so they
   occupy the frame's *height* with a rhythm, and the string is a line of drawing. A still life does not
   have to be a row on a counter.
2. **Value separation by EDGE, not by shadow.** Against a near-black void each object is separated by its
   own contour. This is the cheapest honest move in SVG: a hard silhouette on a dark wall does more work
   than ten gradients. Almost no cast shadow is needed because the light is diffuse.
3. **A frame within the frame.** The niche's lip crops the objects top and bottom. The picture has a
   hole in it, and the hole is the room.

**AVOID.** The devotional reading. This was painted by a Carthusian for a cell; its black is a monk's
black. The brief wants a household, not an oratory — take the geometry and leave the sanctity. Also
avoid the "floating fruit" version: Cotán's objects are *hung*, weighted, and the strings are drawn.

**FOR 01.** Take suspension plus hard silhouette, replace the niche with the edge of the shutter's shadow.

**TEST.** At 400 px, is the black a *shape* with an edge, and are the objects legible without any shading?
If the background is a flat fill, you have the mood and not the mechanism.

## A02 · Francisco de Zurbarán — Still Life with Lemons, Oranges and a Rose

**1633 · Norton Simon Museum, Pasadena · LANE 01, also 04 (drapery)**

**See.** Four objects on a ledge, low and frontal, against a wall almost the same value as the ledge. A
silver plate of lemons, a basket of oranges, a rose. A narrow pale band separates the tabletop from the
darkness above. Light from the left; each object has exactly one lit plane and one dark plane and
nothing else. The whole thing is about the size of a window.

**STEAL.**
1. **Four objects, no more.** The count *is* the composition. The brief's inventory has seven things; if
   all seven get equal weight the picture is a diagram. Give three the light and let four be subordinate.
2. **The register line.** A hard horizontal where the counter meets the void. One line makes an entire
   room: below it is a surface, above it is a wall, and the darkness above is a *thing* with a temperature.
3. **Cold metal against warm fruit.** A small area of the opposite temperature is what makes the warm
   area read as warm. The silver plate is doing that work.

**AVOID.** The waxiness. Zurbarán polishes to a devotional shine and a vector copy of that shine becomes
plastic. Our objects are salt-bloomed and used. Also avoid symmetrical product-shot placement: this is an
inventory, not a catalogue page.

**FOR 01.** Decide the register line first — it is the counter's front edge, low across the frame — and
let the wall above be an *event of light*, not a gradient.

**TEST.** Squint: is the register line still there, and are three objects still lit? A smooth wall gradient
means it has already become decoration.

## A03 · Diego Velázquez — The Waterseller of Seville

**c.1618–22 · Apsley House (Wellington Collection), London · LANE 01 (light events), also 04**

**See.** Two men and a boy. In the foreground a huge earthenware jar, a glass of water with a fig in it,
and a drop of water on the glass. The glass is the brightest thing in the picture. The old man's face is
half in shadow, a single band of light crossing his cheek.

**STEAL.**
1. **The glass as the brightest event, not the fruit.** Water is the only transparent material in the
   inventory, and in painting a transparent thing is a light engine: the rim brightens, the body goes
   dark, the light refracts and lands as a caustic on the counter. One small glass can carry the whole
   value structure — which is exactly what BRIEF §3 asks of 01.
2. **The fig in the glass.** A small opaque mass inside a transparent one is what makes transparency
   visible. Pure water in a vector drawing reads as a grey shape unless something is inside it.
3. **The half-lit face.** Light cutting *across* a form, not around it.

**AVOID.** The anecdote (three ages of man, a genre story). And avoid Velázquez's late loose handling: at
this scale, loose strokes are a lie about a brush you do not have.

**FOR 01.** The glass of water is your single brightest event and your one chance at refraction. Spend it.

**TEST.** Is there a place where a transparent material touches an opaque one and *both* change value?
If not, the glass is a decal.

## A04 · Antonio López García — Madrid from Torres Blancas; with Isabel Quintanilla's interiors

**López: worked and re-worked over two decades · Quintanilla: the madrileño realist interiors · LANE 01, warning for 04**

**See.** López builds a painting from life with a plumb line and a measured perspective, returning to it
for years; surfaces accrue grime, the perspective is corrected and re-corrected, and no edge is stylised.
Quintanilla paints the domestic room as a light-trap: an open doorway, a strip of sun on the floor, a
chair, the rest of the room in warm half-light.

**STEAL.**
1. **Measurement under the surface.** His certainty comes from a geometry you can feel and cannot see.
   In SVG that means a construction grid you build and then delete, leaving only the evidence: the counter
   is 1 unit high and 3 wide, the shutter blade has a real angle in degrees, the fan's cage is a real
   ellipse in perspective.
2. **Grime as a record of time.** The brief's salt bloom on concrete is exactly this: the wall is a
   history, not a colour.
3. **Quintanilla's light strip.** One blade of sun across a wall is enough to say "afternoon" and
   "interior" at once, with no props at all.

**AVOID.** Hyper-detail as a goal. López's detail is a by-product of attention, not a finish; a vector
imitation of it looks engraved, not seen. And avoid the sentimental Castilian interior — our country is
tropical. Take the method, refuse the furniture.

**TEST.** Can you find the construction decision that made the composition, and does it hold when you
check one object's height against its width? If everything is eyeballed, the piece will drift.

## A05 · Joaquín Sorolla — the Jávea / Valencia beach paintings, c.1900–1910

**LANE 02**

**See.** An enormous amount of white, and the white is not white: lit cloth is warm cream, shadowed cloth
is lavender-violet, wet sand reflects both, and there is no neutral grey anywhere. Form is described by
spots of colour placed side by side, not blended. The horizon is often cropped out or pushed high; the
framing is that of a snapshot (Sorolla was steeped in photography from his youth, worked in a
photographer's studio, and posed for photographers himself).

**STEAL.**
1. **Shadows are COLOUR, not darkness.** On white cloth Sorolla uses violet and mauve — a cold hue held
   at a *high* value — which is why his light feels hot. Ruling for 02: no shadow in this painting may be
   a neutral grey. In SVG, tint every shadow shape with a hue and let it stay light but saturated.
2. **Optical mixture at distance.** Two adjacent saturated spots read as a third colour from three metres
   away. Adjacent blurred shapes give you this for free — do *not* lay a smoothing gradient over the
   transitions. The transition *is* the painting.
3. **Cropping as evidence.** Bodies cut by the canvas edge make the picture feel caught rather than
   composed. A photograph's frame is an accident; paint the accident.

**AVOID.** The postcard. Sorolla's beaches are loved and that love is the danger. Also avoid the
"impressionist shimmer" recipe — a hundred strokes of the same size is a texture, not a light. And his
blue is a violet-leaning cobalt, never a cyan holiday blue.

**TEST.** Desaturate to about 20%: do light and shadow still separate by HUE (warm vs cool) rather than by
value? If everything collapses into one cream, you painted a photograph of white, not Sorolla's white.

## A06 · Sorolla again — Sad Inheritance (Triste herencia)

**1899 · Grand Prix, Paris 1900 · LANE 02 — and the collection's conscience**

**See.** A group of naked disabled children bathing at the Malvarrosa beach, a black-robed figure among
them, and the most beautiful light in Spanish painting spent on a subject that is a state failure. The
beauty is not consolation. It is the mechanism that makes the cruelty visible.

**STEAL.** This painting is the whole argument of TROPICO 2026: *the sun is the debt*. The light is the
national resource and the national debt at the same time. If 02 is only beautiful it is a postcard; if it
is only a critique it is an illustration. Sorolla's answer is to make the beauty do the work: the water is
gorgeous and the children are not being helped. That is the standard for the collection — every piece must
carry a cost inside its pleasure.

**AVOID.** Suffering as an aesthetic. Neither the children nor our people are props. The brief's
"unidealised, never posed for the viewer" is the same rule Sorolla keeps least badly: do not paint pity,
paint weather and work.

**TEST.** Name the cost of this sunlight in this frame in one sentence, without using the word "beauty".
If you cannot, the painting is decorative.

## A07 · Athos Bulcão — the Brasília azulejo panels (with Oscar Niemeyer)

**late 1950s onward · e.g. the Igrejinha de Fátima, Brasília · LANE 03**

**See.** Blue and white tiles. A single module — often one square with one simple figure — repeated across
a whole wall, with variation produced by rotation and permutation, not by drawing a picture. No centre, no
border, no focal point: the pattern is a *skin* on architecture. Bulcão described it as participatory: the
wall is a system the eye completes.

**STEAL.**
1. **Module plus permutation rule.** This is the correct answer to "geometric abstraction in SVG". Do not
   draw four hundred shapes; define one module, a small set of allowed states, and a deterministic rule.
   SVG has no randomness at render time, so precompute the sequence into the source and keep it readable.
   The discipline of a rule is what stops the piece becoming wallpaper.
2. **Flat blue on white, no gradients.** The tile is a material; the grout lines are the drawing.
3. **The wall continues past the frame.** The pattern is not composed to fit the canvas — it is a sample
   of a system that runs on.

**AVOID.** Wallpaper — the named failure for 03. Bulcão's panels are architecture: they have a job, a
scale and a light. A pattern with no job is decoration. Also avoid "random scatter": the permutation must
read as a rule or it reads as noise.

**TEST.** Can you state the rule in one sentence — and can you find one place where the rule is
deliberately broken? Uniformity is not a system. A system needs a decision.

## A08 · Lina Bo Bardi / Paulo Mendes da Rocha — Paulista concrete; and Le Corbusier's brise-soleil

**SESC Pompéia, São Paulo, 1977–86; Chandigarh, 1951–65 · LANE 03**

**See.** Bo Bardi kept an old factory and punched round holes through a concrete wall so light arrives as a
dotted field; raw concrete shows the grain of the wooden formwork. Mendes da Rocha builds a single enormous
concrete span and the void beneath it. Le Corbusier's brise-soleil is a screen whose only product is
shadow: in the tropics the sun is vertical and the screen exists to make pattern on the floor.

**STEAL.**
1. **Formwork grain.** Concrete remembers the wood it was poured against. In SVG that is a fine directional
   texture (a pattern at 4–8 px, opacity under .3) inside the concrete mass — the cheapest way to make a
   flat fill read as a material.
2. **Light as a punched hole.** A hole in a wall that lets light through is a stronger composition device
   than a gradient sky, because it is architecture *and* it casts.
3. **The brise-soleil's shadow.** The floor pattern can be the subject. Design the shadow as carefully as
   the building.

**AVOID.** Brutalism as a mood — grey, heavy, sad. In the tropics concrete is bleached, stained and hot;
the piece should be pale and glaring, not dark. And avoid the "architecture render" look: no perfect
white, no hero wide-angle, no people-shaped scale figures.

**TEST.** Does the concrete have a direction — formwork, stain, salt? Can you tell from the building's
shadow alone which way the sun is coming from?

## A09 · Caravaggio — The Calling of St Matthew; Artemisia Gentileschi — Judith Slaying Holofernes

**1599–1600, San Luigi dei Francesi, Rome · 1612–13, Capodimonte, Naples · LANE 04**

**See.** Caravaggio: a dark room, a shaft of light entering on the same diagonal as Christ's pointing hand,
five figures caught mid-motion. **The light does the pointing.** Artemisia: two women and a man on a bed, a
white sheet, and the light falling on the *arms and hands* — on the labour — not on the faces.

**STEAL.**
1. **The light vector is the gesture.** Find the action in the picture and make the light travel along it.
   This is the most useful rule available for 04: the official's act (reading, stamping, waiting) must be
   the direction the light comes from.
2. **Artemisia's task light.** Light falls where the work is, not where the face is. In 04 that means the
   hands and the paper are lit and the face is a half-shape. It is how you paint a bureaucrat without
   painting a portrait.
3. **One big high-value, low-saturation mass as a value anchor** (her white sheet): the eye needs one thing
   to measure the darkness against. Ours can be a wall, a shirt, a form.

**AVOID.** The costume drama. It is 2026, not 1600 — no velvet, no plume, no skull. And avoid the
single-hard-spotlight reading of tenebrism (that is the video-game version): Caravaggio's light is soft at
the edges and his darks are *painted*, not filled.

**TEST.** Cover the light source with your thumb: is the composition still legible? Uncover it: is the
brightest edge on the thing that matters? If the light lands on the face and the story is in the hands,
move the light.

## A10 · The Neobaroque — Lezama Lima, Sarduy, Wifredo Lam

**La expresión americana, 1957 · Barroco, 1974 · The Jungle, 1943 (MoMA) · LANE 04 — the correction**

**See.** Lezama's "señor barroco": the American baroque is not European gloom, it is abundance — the poor
official who is ceremonious, the form stamped twice, the room over-furnished with nothing. Sarduy: the
baroque is the fold, the excess, the sign that knows it is a sign. Lam: a jungle of cane and limbs, figures
assembled from signs (mask, leaf, hand), hybrid bodies in flat hard colour.

**STEAL.**
1. **Baroque as excess of PROCEDURE, not of ornament.** The official's world is baroque because one glass
   of water needs three signatures. You can paint bureaucracy as baroque with no gold at all: repetition,
   doubling, stamps, a queue that folds back on itself.
2. **The fold.** One folded thing in the composition that catches the light twice — lit plane, shadowed
   plane, lit plane — is the purest baroque mechanism available in SVG, because a fold is just three
   shapes and three values.
3. **Lam's totem.** A figure assembled from signs rather than anatomy; useful for a body seen from behind
   or half in shadow, where anatomy is not the point.

**AVOID.** The tropical-baroque cliché: gold, parrots, colonial nostalgia, Carmen Miranda. The tropics
here are concrete and debt, not gilded. And avoid the grotesque: Lam's figures are composite, not
monstrous; the official must remain a person.

**TEST.** What is doubled or folded in the picture? If nothing is repeated with a difference, it is a
chiaroscuro portrait, not a baroque.

## A11 · The thermal camera (FLIR palettes: Ironbow, Rainbow, White hot, Black hot, Lava)

**non-painter reference · LANE 05, and the right wing of 07**

**See.** A false-colour image where colour is temperature. Ironbow runs black→purple→red→orange→yellow→
white; White hot is greyscale; Rainbow is a full spectrum. Automatic gain control rescales the frame to the
hottest and coldest thing in it, so the palette is always *relative*: nothing in a thermal image has an
absolute colour.

**STEAL.**
1. **Relativity as the subject.** This is the intellectual gold: the image is a measurement with a moving
   zero. A street at noon has almost no thermal contrast — the whole world is one temperature, and the only
   hot things are bodies, engines, and the inside of a wall that has been in the sun. So thermal sees the
   tropics *worse* than the eye does, except at the edges of the day. **The state's view of the street is
   blind at noon.** That is a painting.
2. **The palette is a lookup table, not a paint.** The hand appears in the gain and the span, nowhere else.
   Restrain it: three hues plus black and white — or it becomes a video-game HUD.
3. **Thermal does not pass through glass** (ordinary glass is opaque to long-wave infrared). A window is a
   blank in a thermal image. The interior is invisible to the instrument looking at the street. Use that.

**AVOID.** "Predator vision" — hot white bodies, deep blue ground, sci-fi. And avoid thermal as a filter
laid over a normal painting: the image must be organised *by the sensor* (flat where the world is flat, hot
where a body is) or it is a Photoshop layer with a costume.

**FOR 05.** Decide what the sensor cannot see, and make that the subject.

**TEST.** Does the image have a moving zero — one hottest thing that justifies every other value? If the
palette is evenly spread, the sensor is decoration.

## A12 · The surveillance still / the poor image — Hito Steyerl, Trevor Paglen, the CCTV frame

**"In Defense of the Poor Image", 2009 · Limit Telephotography · LANE 05**

**See.** The CCTV still is a format: fisheye or wide, fixed high angle, timestamp burned into a corner,
low bitrate, chroma bleeding, 4:2:0 macroblocks, and a frame chosen by software (motion), not by a person.
Paglen photographs classified sites from the limit of the lens — heat shimmer, a blob, and the knowledge
that the blob is a building.

**STEAL.**
1. **Compression is a material.** JPEG chroma subsampling makes colour bleed two pixels sideways;
   macroblocking puts 8×8 grids in flat areas; interlacing gives horizontal lines. These are *paint*
   behaviours and they are nearly free in SVG: an 8-pixel grid pattern at low opacity in the darks, a
   chromatic offset of one edge by 2–3 px, a scan line every 4 px. Use ONE, not all three.
2. **The burned-in timestamp** is the most politically loaded object in the frame — but the brief forbids
   legible text. So: the timestamp as an unreadable block of vector marks, the *format* of text without the
   information.
3. **The frame chosen by a machine:** off-centre, foot-level, too wide, never composed. The failure of the
   surveillance image is also its poetry — it looks at everything and sees nothing.

**AVOID.** The surveillance aesthetic as a style (green tint, crosshair, REC dot). If it looks like a
hacker film it is a costume. And no fake UI text — the Lead's note for 07 is right for 05 too.

**TEST.** Can you name the artefact, and the institutional reason the camera is where it is? If the
camera's position has no reason, delete the camera.

## A13 · Ernst Haeckel — Kunstformen der Natur

**1899–1904, 100 plates · LANE 06**

**See.** Lithographic plates of radiolaria, medusae, diatoms: grids and fans of specimens at the same
scale, each a perfect radial symmetry, on a blank ground, numbered. The plate is a taxonomy and a
composition at once — the grid is the argument that these forms are related.

**STEAL.**
1. **The plate as a grid of specimens, not a bouquet.** Repetition at one scale with variation is how a
   "data garden" reads as data and not as flowers. Each cell is one "species" of our world — a mango, a fan
   blade, a stamped form — drawn by the same hand at the same scale.
2. **Radial symmetry as a sign of life.** A form that grows from a centre is legible as biological. A form
   that is a rectangle is legible as a machine. The collision of the two is piece 06.
3. **The blank ground.** White space is not empty, it is the museum.

**AVOID.** The ornamental reading. Within a decade these plates were Art Nouveau wallpaper, and that is
exactly the trap: a beautiful garden with no argument is wallpaper. And do not borrow Haeckel's creatures
literally — radiolaria are not plants and this is not the sea.

**FOR 06.** Take the grid, then let the garden grow *through* it — the plate's failure is order; the piece
needs an order that is being invaded.

**TEST.** Count the cells or the axes: is the system legible? Then: is anything breaking it? If nothing
breaks it, it is a pattern. If the system is not legible, it is a mess.

## A14 · Owen Jones — The Grammar of Ornament; with Merian and Marianne North

**1856 · Metamorphosis insectorum Surinamensium, 1705 · North's field paintings, 1871–85 · LANE 06**

**See.** Jones: a hundred chromolithograph plates of ornament from many cultures and 37 propositions, two
of which matter most — **all ornament should be based on a geometrical construction**, and natural forms
must be *conventionalised*: "Flowers or other natural objects should not be used as ornaments, but
conventional representations founded upon them, sufficiently suggestive to convey the intended image to the
mind." Merian: a plant drawn as a stage — stem, leaf, fruit and the insect's whole life cycle on the same
plant, a composite of moments. North: botanical paintings made in the field, hot and specific, showing the
plant's *habit* (how it grows) rather than its prettiness.

**STEAL.**
1. **Conventionalise.** A leaf in a vector painting should be a *rule* for a leaf (a family of curves), not
   a traced leaf. This is the single most important craft rule for 06 and the line between art nouveau and
   clip-art.
2. **Merian's composite time.** One plant carrying several moments at once — bud, flower, fruit, rot. That
   is literally a data garden: a plant that shows its own data, growth, damage, yield.
3. **North's habit.** Draw how the thing grows — the direction of the stem, the weight of the fruit — and
   the picture becomes specific instead of decorative.

**AVOID.** Wallpaper (the two lanes with the most wallpaper risk are 03 and 06) and the "pressed flower"
flatness. Also avoid the innocence of colonial botany: these plates were instruments of empire — the
Wardian case, Kew, the rubber transfer out of Amazonia. A data garden in 2026 is the same apparatus with
better software, and that tension is piece 06's teeth.

**TEST.** Is every natural form a rule, or is one of them a trace? Does the composition carry information —
a stage, a count, a damage — or only beauty?

## Closing notes for 07 (the hero) and for everyone

**The retablo is a frame that flatters everything inside it.** Its risk, named in the Lead's own concept
note, is a design board. The panel structure must be justified by an argument — three ways of knowing one
street — not by taste. If a panel exists because the shape needed filling, remove the shape.

**The predella is A13.** Seven small squares quoting the seven pieces is exactly Haeckel's grid: same
scale, same hand, variation as the argument. And like Haeckel, it must be *invaded* by one thing that does
not obey the grid — an object from the street that appears in no inventory.

**The wings are A09 + A11.** Left wing: Artemisia's task light (the light on the hands, the room dark).
Right wing: the thermal view, relative palette, three hues, and the window that is a blank. They are the
same instant seen by two instruments; the craft test is that the shadow directions agree.

**The centre panel is A05 + A02.** Sorolla's coloured shadows and Zurbarán's register line: the malecón at
16:40 with a hard horizontal (the sea wall) and a value anchor (the sea, or a white wall) to measure the
light against.

## The opposition — what we are not

These are the visual clichés this collection must not accidentally become. If a piece starts to look like
one of these, that is the finding.

- **AI gloss** — the soft-focus, high-saturation, over-smoothed "midjourney sheen". Symptom: nothing is
  dirty, every edge glows, the palette is uniformly vivid.
- **Synthwave / neon dusk** — the wrong 2026. The brief allows neon only where the piece is *about* the
  grid (05), and even there the neon must be an instrument colour, not a mood.
- **Tropicana kitsch** — palm, sunset, flamingo, cocktail. The country we are painting has a container port
  and a blackout.
- **Cyberpunk rust-neon** — rust and cyan as decoration. Rust is a material with a history; it is not an
  accent.
- **Generative gradient mesh** — the "pretty blob". A shape with no edge law and no argument.
- **Golden hour** — the most photographed light in the world. Our light is noon or a shutter blade.
- **Poverty tourism** — the documentary gaze that makes the subject beautiful and powerless at once. Our
  people are working, not suffering for the camera.
- **The infographic** — labels, arrows, legends, a title doing the work. The painting must not explain
  itself. (The brief's rule: no legible text.)
- **The filtered photograph** — a photo with a paint filter, which is neither a painting nor a document.

If a piece reminds you of a reference above, fine. If it reminds you of a reference above and of nothing
else, it is a copy.
