# SEMILLA — argument, rule, pass log

**Artist:** aereo · **Idiom:** lithographed seed-packet illustration, c. 1920 · **Canvas:** 1600×1200

## Argument (written before drawing)

What this piece knows that the caption will not say: *a seed packet is a promise about a future that
the packet cannot show*. It shows one specimen, four stages, a calendar and a set of instructions,
and asks you to believe the plant will arrive. Mine is a packet for a plant that is infrastructure —
its stem is a lattice mast, its leaves are solar panels with circuit veins and red indicator lights,
its flower is a dish cluster — and the four stages the packet promises are not botanical stages but
network stages: seed, sprout, router, tower. The specimen has already outgrown the packet: its mast
leaves through the top frame and the title band, tearing them. The packet's ornamental border is a
scroll-and-curl vine; if you look under the soil, the root system is the same module, rotated and
halved. The packet's own decoration is what the plant is rooted in.

## Generative rule

**The plant grows through four network stages (seed, sprout, router, tower), and the packet's border
vine and the specimen's root system are one module** — the same S-scroll-with-curl path, repeated at
two scales and two orientations; the growth stages are the module mutating, the roots are the
ornament misread as biology, and the calendar ring counts the months the packet promises.

## Pass log

**Pass 1.** Full packet: frame, interrupted teal title band, specimen mast, soil, roots, stage
vignettes, calendar ring, pictograms, stamps, colour bars, lot block. Saw: a packet immediately, but
the plant read as a tower with propeller blades, the soil was a black slab that swallowed the roots,
and the frame's ornament was so light it read as a squiggle.

**Pass 2.** Leaves rebuilt as pointed botanical leaves with a midrib, paired veins and red LED dots
on the veins; frame and title band torn where the mast passes; soil turned into a hatched section so
ink roots can read; roots and border set in the same deep teal so the rhyme is findable. Saw: the
specimen became a plant; the roots read; but the middle of the plate was empty and the left title
half was blank.

**Pass 3.** Added the magnified seed detail (a botanical-plate detail circle with the chip inside,
led by a dotted line to the seed cut-away); lot block and tallies in the left title band; stones in
the section. Saw: the composition filled; the detail circle became a second focal point close in
weight to the plant.

## Checkpoint at pass 3

- **FAR (400 px): READS.** A lithographed seed packet: cream paper, teal band with a title, scroll
  border, a pale specimen, a dark soil band, an instrument panel. The idiom is immediate.
  **Failing:** the plant is a thin vertical at thumbnail; the packet's weight is all in the frame and
  the title, so at 400 px the specimen is the weakest thing in its own packet.
- **MIDDLE (1600 px): MOSTLY READS.** The specimen's leaves are leaves and its stem is a mast; the
  four stage vignettes are legible; the calendar ring is legible. **Failing:** the middle of the plate
  is empty and the roots are still too regular.
- **NEAR (100%): PAYS.** Leaf veins with red nodes, the chip inside the magnified seed, the root curls
  repeating the border vine, the stamps' tick rings, the printer's colour bars and lot block.

**Pass 4.** Roots varied (scale, angle, branching) and one root made to escape the packet at the lower
left; stones and strata lines in the section; the topsoil edge strengthened; three berries on the
mast.

**Pass 5.** Fixed the root transforms (a mirror had turned half the root system upward, into weeds)
and rebalanced the section. Converged at five passes — see the honesty note below.

## Three-distance results (final)

- **FAR:** a seed packet, unmistakably: framed card, title band, specimen, section, instruments.
- **MIDDLE:** a plant that is a mast with solar leaves and a dish crown, four growth stages, a
  twelve-month calendar ring, a magnified seed, certification stamps, colour bars.
- **NEAR:** the border vine and the root system are the same path; leaf veins carry red nodes; the
  seed's chip shows through its cut-away; every stamp and tally is a mark rather than a letter.

## Honest self-critique

- **I did not reach the twelve passes this room asks for.** E10 converged at five: three structural
  passes, a detail pass and a repair pass. The piece is complete and holds at three distances, but it
  is a shallower work than E1 and E8, and the budget shows.
- **The plant is legible as a plant, not yet as infrastructure.** The mast is a lattice and the
  leaves are panels, but at arm's length the specimen reads as a stylised plant with a radio mast
  behind it rather than as one organism that is both. The four stage vignettes carry that idea better
  than the specimen does.
- **The detail circle competes with the specimen.** It is the same size as the plant's crown and sits
  at the optical centre, so it takes the eye that the specimen should hold.
- **The roots/border rhyme is real but quiet.** The modules match in shape and colour, and one root
  escapes the packet, but the correspondence needs a viewer who compares the bottom border with the
  soil. It is a reward for looking, not a discovery that announces itself.
- What works: the packet is convincing as an object (frame, band, section, stamps, colour bars, lot
  block); the tear where the mast leaves is the piece's one act of violence; the calendar ring is the
  best small drawing in the room's three pieces; the colour bars and lot block give the near distance
  a printer's reality.
