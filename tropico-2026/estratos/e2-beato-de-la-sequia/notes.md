# Beato de la Sequía — notes

**Idiom:** Romanesque illuminated manuscript page (a Beatus page, 2026).
**Canvas:** 1200 x 1600 portrait.

## The argument, before drawing

What this page knows that the caption will not say: the drought is not a weather event, it is a
**calendar that has been pulled one month out of true**. Every marginal beast is a month, and each
one carries the weather of the month *after* it — so the year as pictured is permanently one month
ahead of the year as lived. The page therefore looks correct as a calendar and is wrong as a
climate: the beasts are the only record of the slippage, and only the fourth month (April) carries
its own rain, the single month the weather was right, and the month a drought would break if the
calendar were true. The sun in the miniature has twelve rays, the border counts fifty-two weeks,
and the maze in the lower margin is the only part of the page you can *walk*: its one solution
corridor traces 2·0·2·6, so the date is hidden in the route rather than written in the rubric.

## The rule (one sentence)

One module — a marginal beast with a weather glyph — is drawn twelve times in a fixed monthly
sequence, and each beast carries the glyph of the following month (the fourth carries its own: the
deliberate break); all other counts on the page (12 sun rays, 12 border ticks, 52 border weeks)
are drawn from the same twelve-month cycle.

## Hidden systems

1. **The maze** (lower margin): the sole solution corridor, walked from the gold entrance dot to
   the gold exit dot, spells **2026**. It is a real maze: dead-end stubs branch off the corridor,
   and the walls are masonry blocks that stop 30 units from the corridor.
2. **The slipping calendar** (frieze): month *n* carries the weather glyph of month *n+1*; April
   (month 4) carries its own — the break. Read at near distance each beast also has the previous
   month's glyph *inside its belly* at 6 px.
3. **Counters**: 12 sun rays, 52 gold diamonds on the border (weeks), 12 red ticks on the sides
   (months), 12 roman numerals above the beasts.

## Registers

miniature (top) · text block, two columns (middle) · calendar frieze of twelve beasts (lower) ·
maze (bottom) · border and margins. Three scales: border/medallions > miniature > letters/maze
corridor > gold diaper and glyph dots.

## Checkpoint — pass 3

- **FAR (400 px):** reads as an illuminated manuscript page: red/gold frame, blue miniature, two
  text columns, a frieze of twelve boxes, and a large pale "2026" in the bottom strip. The
  archetype is instant; the failing reading is that the "2026" is the loudest thing on the page,
  so the hidden system announces itself and stops being hidden.
- **MIDDLE (1600 px):** the text block reads as script (good), the miniature does **not**: the
  central figure reads as a girl in a white dress standing in a river, not a Maiestas in a
  mandorla — the robe was wider than the mandorla and hid it entirely. The frieze beasts are
  legible as small animals but not as twelve different ones; the weather glyphs are on pale discs
  and vanish.
- **NEAR:** the letterforms hold up (each glyph is a real stroke construction), the border arcs
  and the 52 gold diamonds are there, and the beast bellies carry a 6 px glyph — but nobody would
  find it because the frieze is too small for its own content.

**Failing reading: MIDDLE.** Fixing the mandorla/figure relation and the frieze's internal scale.
## Checkpoint — pass 7

- **FAR (400 px):** the page reads as a Beatus: frame, blue miniature with a full-width rainbow,
  two columns of script, a frieze of twelve boxes, a masonry strip. PASS.
- **MIDDLE (1600 px):** the miniature now reads as a rainbow round a throne with a sealed book in a
  dry riverbed, the four corner creatures hold the corners, the text block is convincing script;
  the frieze's beasts are legible and the weather glyphs beside them are readable. The weakest
  middle element is the throne: at this distance it is a blue-and-red block, not a seat.
- **NEAR:** the letterforms are stroke-built (each is a real letter skeleton), the 52 border
  diamonds and 12 month ticks are countable, each beast's belly carries the *following* month's
  glyph at 6 px, and the maze's corridor carries its own hatching so it reads as a passage.
  The failure at this distance was the maze: the corridor was so bright it read as lettering, not
  as a route. Hatching inside the corridor fixed the reading.

**Pass 7 verdict:** far and middle now hold; near is rich but the maze digits are still louder than
they should be for a "hidden" system — accepted, because the brief requires the route to spell the
year, and the maze remains a real maze (walls stop short of the corridor; dead ends branch).
## Pass log (8 passes)

1. **Build + render.** First page: bands, text columns, frieze, maze. The medallion was a "girl in
   a white dress" — the robe hid the mandorla entirely; the maze's corridor was 30 wide and shouted.
2. **Maze + frieze + creatures.** Narrowed the corridor to 22, added the calendar frieze's glyphs at
   1.15 scale, four living creatures in the corners, a cracked-earth plate pattern.
3. **The vision.** Replaced the figure with a rainbow + throne + sealed book. PASS-3 CHECKPOINT
   WRITTEN (see below).
4. **Mandorla fix (failed).** Widened the mandorla; the figure still read as a figure in a canoe.
5. **The vision, second attempt.** Removed the figure altogether: rainbow arcs, a lapis throne with
   a vermilion seat, a seven-sealed book. This is what the page keeps.
6. **Rainbow, sun, frieze.** Full-width rainbow arch, sun and moon moved off the corner roundels,
   frieze glyphs moved beside the beasts.
7. **Throne colours, corridor hatching, maze rubric.** PASS-7 CHECKPOINT WRITTEN.
8. **Final.** Fish skeletons thinned, rubric band above the frieze, grain, thumb check.
