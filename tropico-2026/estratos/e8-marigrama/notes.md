# MARIGRAMA — argument, rule, pass log

**Artist:** aereo · **Idiom:** tide chart / marigram, 19th-c. hydrographic engraving · **Canvas:** 1600×1200

## Argument (written before drawing)

What this piece knows that the caption will not say: *a tide chart is a mountain range that happens
to be water*. A year of high waters plotted day by day is a silhouette of peaks and passes — springs
are ridges, neaps are cols — and once you see it as a range you cannot unsee it. But the range is
drawn over a harbour, and the harbour is the negative space under it: the roofline of sheds, silos,
cranes and a lighthouse is the lower edge of the water, so every gap in the mountain is a building
standing in the tide. One month has no observations: the range simply stops, and in that slot the
lighthouse stands in a hole in the water with the rust rule of the missing month drawn through it.
The plate is a cross-section of a coast whose datum is a quay.

## Generative rule

**One sine module of 12.42 hours, sampled once a day and folded into twelve monthly ridges:** the
spring–neap envelope (14.77 d) is the mass of the range, the daily sample of the semidiurnal module
is the serrated crest, the year's longer swing and one storm give the peaks their unequal heights,
and the low-water envelope is not a tide at all — it is the harbour's skyline.

## Pass log

**Pass 1.** Plate, border, tide curve, harbour, marginal scale, module inset, tallies, cartouche.
Saw: an old chart, immediately — but the range was a picket fence (every spike identical) and the
harbour read as a wall.

**Pass 2.** Rebuilt the tide as an envelope rather than a raw sample; gave the skyline height
variation, gaps and a breakwater; made the water hatching visible; marked the missing month inside
the range. Saw: still a comb — the harbour's roofline was hiding the neap passes.

**Pass 3.** Rebalanced the vertical structure (range 150–396, skyline 470–650, datum 700) so the
neap lows clear the roofs. Saw: the passes appeared, but every ridge was a needle of the same height.

## Checkpoint at pass 3

- **FAR (400 px): READS.** A serrated dark mass across the plate over a pale harbour skyline, with
  instruments below. Unmistakably a chart of something that rises and falls. **Failing:** the range
  reads as a comb, not a mountain range — the peaks are identical, so the eye reads data, not terrain.
- **MIDDLE (1600 px): PARTLY READS.** The tide is legible; the harbour is legible as a harbour
  (silos, cranes, lighthouse); the missing month is a pale slot with a rust rule. **Failing:** the
  skyline is flat-bottomed and evenly spaced — a terrace of warehouses, not a port.
- **NEAR (100%): THIN.** Hatching, windows, crane cables and the tide scale resolve, but there is
  little that only resolves at 100% — the near distance was just the middle at a larger size.

**Pass 4.** Split the mass into a smooth spring–neap ridge and a serrated crest (the daily sample),
which gave the range a hairy silhouette instead of a clean zigzag.

**Pass 5.** Compressed the tide scale so the neap lows sit above the roofs; added the second inset —
the 60-day envelope with its 14.77-day brace and the spring peaks marked — which makes the rule
itself part of the plate.

**Pass 6.** Unequal peaks: a longer annual swing, the lunar declination cycle and one storm (a
September surge) so the range has a massif; crest serration tripled.

**Pass 7.** Valley levels varied; windows in the warehouses, cables on the cranes, the breakwater
rubble, month ticks along the top margin and a rust pointer at the missing month.

## Checkpoint at pass 7

- **FAR:** A mountain range over a harbour, with a hole in the range at the missing month. The
  comb is gone; the silhouette now has a massif and unequal ridges. **Failing:** at 400 px the two
  insets and the tallies merge into one pale band of marginalia; only the cartouche survives.
- **MIDDLE:** The tide, the port and the instruments all read at once; the storm massif is the
  dominant peak, which is right.
- **NEAR:** Warehouse windows, crane cables, the lighthouse's hatching, the rust "no observations"
  rule, the tallies and the brace on the envelope. It pays, but modestly.

## Three-distance results (final)

- **FAR:** a chart plate: dark serrated range, pale harbour below, instruments beneath. Reads.
- **MIDDLE:** the rule is legible — spring ridges and neap passes, a missing month, a datum line, a
  scale, an inset that explains the module.
- **NEAR:** the harbour resolves into roof detail and rigging; the missing month is hatched, ruled
  and pointed at; the inset's brace measures the 14.77-day period.

## Honest self-critique

- **The mountain metaphor is half-won.** A marigram is periodic by nature, so a year of it is a comb;
  I added height variation and a storm to make it a range, and it now has a massif, but at 400 px it
  still reads more as a serrated data edge than as terrain. The honest version of this piece would
  have let the crest meander (weather, river flow, surge) much more than the physics allows.
- **The harbour is a terrace, not a port.** The buildings are evenly spaced along the datum with a
  repeating sawtooth roof module; only the lighthouse and the silos break the rhythm. A real quay has
  long low sheds and sudden tall things; mine is regular because I generated it from a list.
- **The marginalia are stronger than the main image.** The two insets, the tallies and the cartouche
  are the best part of the plate and they are all at the bottom. The composition is therefore
  bottom-heavy: the range has no counterweight in its sky.
- **The missing month is the piece's only event.** It works (a pale slot, a rust rule, a pointer, a
  hatched no-data field), but everything else in the plate is system without incident.
- What works: the far read is genuinely a chart; the negative-space harbour is a real double reading
  (water above, buildings below, one silhouette serving both); the serrated crest carries the daily
  module; the instruments make the rule explicit without a single sentence.
