Seven views of the República de Trópico in the year 2026 — the near future that has already arrived, and is already weathering. Painted by hand in SVG, by a team of agents, one art language at a time.

“El sol es la deuda.” — The sun is the debt.

WALL TEXT / 01

The fiction. Eight degrees north. A tourism economy, a container port, blackouts, a Ministry of the Future that survives mostly as a stencil on a wall, and a sun that is simultaneously the national resource and the national debt. Nothing in these paintings is science fiction. Everything in them is 2026: solar panels, remittances, cables, salt bloom, heat, debt, mangoes, a machete, a fan.

The method. The collection begins in Spanish realism — the bodegón as a ledger — and then refuses to stay there. Each following piece re-looks at the same country through an entirely different visual language, until the country is only recognisable by its objects, its light, and its weather. The gallery is ordered by the order in which the languages were learned.

PROCESS / 02
  1. ROUND AThree languages, one country. Spanish-realist bodegón as a ledger of the household; Sorolla's noon on the malecón; tropical-modernist poster for a ministry that has already aged.
  2. CRITIQUEThe curator interrupts. All three scored below the ship line, and the verdict was about the collection, not the pieces: one repeating composition skeleton, drifting accents, and no painting in which the sun actually hurts.
  3. ROUND BThree more languages. Neo-baroque tenebrism in a container yard; a thermal surveillance frame at 03:00; a botanical plate for a plant that does not exist.
  4. FIX PASSESEach piece sent back once. Not to be repainted — to solve the one fault the critic named by place: a translucent figure, a flat shadow, a circle pretending to be a mango.
  5. SYNTHESISThe retablo. The collection closes as a three-ink screenprint: the private room, the threshold, the state's thermal eye, and the inventory printed beneath like a register of saints.
