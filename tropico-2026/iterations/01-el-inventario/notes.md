# NOTES — 01 EL INVENTARIO

Artist: maestro · Spanish realism / bodegón · SVG, viewBox 0 0 1600 1200
Files: `el-inventario.svg` · `render-el-inventario.png` (1600 px) · this file · `meta.json`
Checks also present: `thumb-check.png` (400 px) and `gray-check.png` (400 px greyscale).

## CONCEPT

The inventory of a household economy laid out on a concrete counter the way a
sacristy lays out the vessels: mango, jar of instant coffee, solar charge controller
with its cable, machete, folded stamped form, glass of water, one plastic fan blade.
Frontal, grave, warm, quiet. A ledger, not a display.

Two decisions that could have gone wrong:

1. **The glass of water is the brightest event, not the fruit.** Velázquez's
   waterseller glass (REFS A03): the rim brightens, the body goes dark, the light
   refracts and lands as a caustic on the stone. The mango stays warm and mid-value.
   The eye goes to the glass first, then travels left to the fruit.
2. **The light is a pocket low on the wall, and the three standing objects are
   silhouettes against it** (Cotán: value separation by edge against near-black;
   Zurbarán: the register line low across the frame). Their cast shadows fall on the
   lit wall, which is what makes them stand in a room rather than on a shelf.

## LIGHT

One sun, off-frame upper right, through a shuttered window. On the back wall the sun
lands as a hard-edged pocket whose upper boundary is the shutter's edge; the counter
takes the same light as a pool running in from the right. Every cast shadow points
left and slightly back. Shadow is cool (violet-grey wash over the dark); light is warm
ochre. Hot events: glass rim, water line, caustic, the machete's spine.

Value plan: 1 light (wall pocket + sunlit counter), 2 mid (shadowed counter, jar,
paper, plastic sheet), 3 dark (upper wall, object shadow-sides, machete), 4 near-black
(void under the ledge, corners). Brightest event: glass. Second: the caustic.

## PASS LOG (what I saw, what I changed)

**Pass 1 — flat value block-in.** Rendered and looked. Saw: a transparent gap at the
wall/counter junction printed as a white line across the whole canvas; the fan blade
was a giant white egg; the mango a plastic orange; objects spaced in an even row like a
display; the bottom fifth dead flat black; the machete floating with no wall shadow.
Changed: everything. Raised the counter, re-cut the planes, laid the machete down.

**Pass 2 — full recompose.** Saw: legible anatomy of each object but still a CG look —
hard silhouettes, saturated orange lid, a navy "toy radio", the fan still an egg, a
neon-looking bright line on the counter. Changed: killed the saturated accents, built
the light as blades with falloff, added aggregate and scratches.

**Pass 3 — one coherent shadow edge through the picture.** Saw: the light read as a
flat beige triangle pasted on the wall; a hard dark bar of shadow top-right; the pale
mass still competing with the glass; the glass still a pale tube. Changed: cool shadow
wash, the glass darkened, the fan blade made low-key.

**Pass 4 — Cotán mode (near-black wall, small hard wall blade).** Saw: the mood arrived
but the pale arch at the left (the leaning fan blade) read as a tombstone and shouted
louder than the glass; the jar's cast shadow read as a light band. Changed: fan blade
taken off the wall and laid flat; machete laid on the counter; contact shadows
strengthened.

**Pass 5 — machete on the counter, fan blade lying in the light.** Saw: contact better;
but the wall light sat above the objects so the picture was a dark room with furniture
standing outside the sun; the machete read as a pole; the pale form bottom-right
unreadable. Changed: the light brought down onto the wall behind the objects.

**Pass 6 — the light pocket low on the wall; standing objects silhouetted; their
shadows thrown on the lit wall.** Saw: the objects finally sat in a room with coherent
shadows, but they were OUTSIDE the light and went black (jar read as a bucket), the
mango's light side was a hard flat cut, the fan blade still unreadable. Changed: the
pocket widened left to reach the jar's shoulder, the sun pool widened on the counter,
the jar's lit side opened up, the floating stick on the dark wall removed, the glass's
wall shadow softened (it had read as a second object).

**Pass 7 (final) — resolve the last two objects, tune the values.** Saw: fan blade now
fully in frame as a whole paddle with its ribbed root, but it was the palest thing in
the picture; the machete's sheen band read as a metal pipe; the mango's lit crescent
read as a flat cut. Changed: fan sheet dropped to mid-value, machete sheen dimmed and
the spine/edge differentiated, mango's terminator softened and its shadow side lifted
with a warm bounce, jar's tan band reduced so it stops reading as a wooden bucket.

## HONEST SELF-CRITIQUE

The structure is right and the picture is quiet: one light, four value masses, a real
register line, a focal glass, shadows that fall away from the sun, a wall shadow that
puts the objects in a room. It survives at 400 px and it survives greyscale.

But it is **not yet a painting**. The failure is material: the objects are built from
smooth gradient shapes with soft edges, so the mango still wants to be a sphere, the
jar a bucket with a lid, the machete a tube, the plastic sheet a pale slug. Cocina
texture is in the concrete and nowhere else. The paint quality that the brief asks for
(edge break-up, scumble, dry brush, lost and found contours) is present only as a
1–2 px displacement wobble. If I had another two passes they would go entirely into
material truth, not into composition.

Weakest single thing: **the jar of instant coffee** — it reads as a wooden/near-black
bucket, and its label band is a rectangle rather than a thing wrapped round glass.
Second: the mango's shadow side, which is too even a dark mass.

## MATERIAL PASS (after Lead review — surface only, nothing moved)

Instructions were: no recompose, no light change, fix material only, in a fixed order.
What was done:

1. **Mango.** Silhouette broken: one side flattened, one rounder; a stem dimple and
   stub set into the flattened top instead of a geometric oval (a first attempt put a
   horn on the right — visible in the render, reverted). Added a flatter lit facet on
   the upper-right with a ridge line, a warm bounce off the counter low on the shadow
   side (the shadow side is now dark orange-brown, not black), ~30 speckles clustered
   denser on the shadow side, ~8 pale speckles on the lit side, and four pin-prick
   pores (dark dot + light pixel).
2. **Jar.** Now a glass jar: a coffee mass inside with an **uneven top surface** (wavy
   path), a label band that **wraps** (top and bottom edges draw the cylinder's ellipse,
   not a rectangle), a dark screw lid with vertical ridges, and two thin broken glass
   highlights down the body. Removed the broad tan band that made it read as a barrel.
3. **Machete.** Blade tapered toward its point off-frame left; a dark spine band; a
   bevel stripe running 4–8 units in from the cutting edge; the light on the bevel and
   the spine broken into dashes instead of one grey bar; a steel ferrule between blade
   and grip; wood grain in the handle.
4. **Concrete.** A finer grit pattern over the aggregate, a damp dark patch where the
   wall meets the counter and a second softer one to the right, two hairline cracks (one
   branched) with a pale displacement line, and the existing chipped spot on the arris.
5. **Glass.** The water surface is now a 2–3 unit bright line with a darker meniscus
   stroke above it; one vertical refraction break across the body where the counter's
   edge jogs inside the glass.

Honest result: the mango and the jar now carry material; the machete reads as a blade
with a bevel rather than a pipe; the glass's water line is no longer a lozenge. The
mango's silhouette is still a little too broad, and the label band, while wrapping,
reads a touch light. Material truth moved from 3 to 4; everything else is unchanged.
