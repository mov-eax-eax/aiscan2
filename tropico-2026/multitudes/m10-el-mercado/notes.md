# M10 EL MERCADO — notes
Artist: lead · night market · 1600x1200

## THE ARGUMENT
A market at night is a crowd machine. The picture is not about anybody in it: it is about the
density and the light. Every bulb is a small sun with its own pool on the wet ground; the crowd
thins toward the vanishing point so the street reads as depth, not as wallpaper.

## CROWD RULE
86 figures placed procedurally by depth, sorted far-to-near so the near ones occlude the far. Pose
varies through four variants (standing, walking, carrying, arms down) plus hats on every fifth.
Stalls recede in bays of two per side, awnings cycling through four colours. Every figure faces
along the street - nobody looks at the viewer.

## FIND-IT
Nine small red lanterns, one per depth band, at different heights. Not announced in the caption.

## THREE ACTIONS
1. An argument, mid-left: two figures, one with a raised arm.
2. A vendor pouring behind a stall, centre-left, with a visible pour and a cup.
3. A child chasing a cat, lower right.

## PASS LOG
1. First build had the ground plane wrong - the street was a convex mound and the stalls sat in a
   bowl. Rebuilt with a proper horizon (y 545), a converging street polygon, side masses, and
   depth-scaled stalls.
2. Second build: perspective correct, but the sky was empty and the rim lights read as white
   outlines. Added moon, high catenaries and light pools; rim opacity dropped 0.5 -> 0.18.
3. Third: awnings too candy and the street too bright. Desaturated the awning cycle, darkened the
   ground, added kerb lines converging on the vanishing point.
4. Fourth: the scene was atmosphere without incident. Added the three actions explicitly (argument,
   vendor pouring, child and cat) so the middle distance has stories to find.

## HONEST CRITIQUE
The depth and the crowd work; the incident does not, quite. The three actions are small relative to
the crowd and are drawn as silhouettes with gesture rather than as characters with faces, so a
viewer can miss them entirely. The stalls read as coloured billboards rather than as stalls with
structure - no posts, no side panels, no hanging stock. The most successful part is the far half:
the shrinking stalls and the lights converging, which is where the scene actually becomes a place.
