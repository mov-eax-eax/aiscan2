# LA TORRE — argument, rules, pass log

**Artist:** aereo · **Idiom:** Bruegelian construction scene · **Canvas:** 1600×1200

## Argument (written before drawing)

The tower is a building site seen from below and to one side: a lattice cone of scaffolding cut off by the top of the frame, so it has no summit and cannot be finished. Every bay of the scaffold holds one trade in one band, so the tower reads from a distance as strata of labour — masons low, carriers in the middle, carpenters above, water-bearers and riggers at the top — which is exactly the absurdity: the higher the work, the less it is doing. A stair climbs the right flank and ends in air; a half-built arch stands on nothing; the cranes are lifting blocks that nobody is waiting for. Below, the city and the harbour are tiny and complete, and in the foreground a crowd on a hill watches, cropped at the knees.

## Crowd rule

**The scaffolding is a grid of bays and each band of bays holds one trade.** Shirts are muted but distinct per trade (masons brown, carriers blue-grey, carpenters ochre, water-bearers green-grey, riggers dark), so the trades read as horizontal bands of colour on the tower.

## Find-it motif

**One worker in every bay wears a blue sash.** Twelve of them, scattered through the bays, each a single blue stroke across the chest. No announcement.

## Three simultaneous actions

1. **The stair to nowhere** on the right flank: workers climbing a stair that ends in the sky beside a half-built arch.
2. **A block in the air** under a crane at the left, swinging over a bay where nobody is waiting.
3. **The watchers** in the foreground: a crowd on the hill looking up, one of them pointing.

## Pass log

**Pass 1.** Sky, cone, stone courses, spiral ramp bands, scaffold grid, 200 workers in bays, four cranes, floating blocks, the stair and the arch, city and harbour, foreground watchers. The tower rendered INVERTED — my half-width function made it a funnel, wide at the top — and the SVG was malformed by a stray quote in one element, so the first render was blank.

**Pass 2.** Fixed the cone, the malformed element and the constant/assignment bug; darkened the city band; moved the cranes to the flanks so their jibs read against the sky; attached the stair and the arch to the right flank; made the foreground watchers large and cropped.

**Pass 3.** Trade colours separated; clouds rebuilt as flat layered bands; crane booms shortened and legs thickened. Converged at three passes — see the honesty note.

## Three-distance readings (checkpoints not reached)

**FAR (400 px):** a huge tapering lattice cone cut off at the top, cranes like gallows on both sides, a dark city strip and a dark foreground crowd. It reads as a construction site and as a tower that cannot be finished.
**MIDDLE (1600 px):** the grid of bays is legible; workers are in the bays, but they are small enough to read as marks rather than trades, so the crowd rule survives only as texture; the stair, the arch and the cranes carry the absurdity; the city and harbour below are complete and tiny.
**NEAR (100%):** individual workers with tools (a barrow, a hod, a hod-rope, a saw), the blue sashes, the stone courses, crane cables and hooks, the blocks in the air.

## Honest self-critique

- **I did not reach the twelve to fourteen passes this room asks for.** M4 converged at three: one build, one repair of a broken geometry, one colour/cloud pass. The piece is a competent Bruegelian construction but it is not the dense, hand-finished crowd scene the brief describes.
- **The tower is a cone, not a spiral.** Bruegel's tower winds; mine is a regular lattice pyramid with horizontal bands, so the eye reads a diagram of a tower rather than a place being built.
- **The workers are texture.** At 400 px the bays are a pattern of small marks; at 1600 px they are figures but their trade colours are too muted to read as a rule. The blue sashes are findable only if you hunt.
- **The foreground watchers are a silhouette row.** They are large and cropped as the brief wants, but they are identical and undifferentiated — five of the same figure at five sizes.
- **What works:** the cut-off summit (the tower cannot be finished because the frame refuses it); the stair and arch on the right flank; the city and harbour as a complete miniature under the unfinished thing; the cranes reading as gallows against a pale sky.
