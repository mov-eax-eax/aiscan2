# El Jardín — notes

**Idiom:** Mughal miniature. **Canvas:** 1600 x 1200 landscape.

## Argument (before drawing)

What the picture knows that the caption will not say: the garden is a **chahar bagh**, four quadrants
cut by water, and the crowd is the plan made visible. Three quadrants are full — courtiers at the
left, musicians at the right, servants and elephants at the front — and the fourth quadrant, the one
nearest the viewer, is empty except for **one gardener kneeling at a bed**. That emptiness is not a
gap: it is the durbar's arithmetic, the fourth quarter of the garden being held for the ruler's
procession that has not arrived. A drone painted as a bird circles overhead eight times at eight
heights, because the garden is being watched from above and only the birds are wrong.

## Crowd rule

Every figure stands inside one of the four quadrants of the chahar bagh. The quadrant is read from
the figure's feet, not its head. Quadrants I–III hold the durbar (courtiers, musicians, servants,
guards, elephants); quadrant IV holds exactly one figure. A secondary rule: in every group of three
courtiers, one carries a fan.

## Find-it motif

A drone disguised as a bird: **8 occurrences**, at eight heights, spread across the sky (x = 210,
430, 620, 790, 960, 1130, 1290, 1460; y rising from 70 to 300). Each is a bird silhouette; at 100%
the four rotor rings are visible under the wings, and the tallest three are small enough to read as
birds.

## Three simultaneous actions

1. **The audience** on the marble terrace: the ruler seated in the chhatri, a courtier bowing at the
   steps, two attendants with fly-whisks.
2. **The music** in the right quadrant: four musicians on a carpet, two dancers, a drummer with his
   hands raised.
3. **The service** in the left/front: servants carrying trays up the channel; one has dropped a tray
   in the foreground, and a dog is already at it.

## Checkpoint — pass 4

- **FAR (400 px):** a walled garden with a white pavilion at the top, water channels crossing it in
  four, crowds in three quadrants and a bare fourth — the plan (chahar bagh) reads before anything
  else. The birds in the sky read as birds. **PASS**, with one reservation: the pavilion is the
  brightest mass, which is correct, but the wall band is thin, so the top of the picture lacks the
  dark mass that would make the garden glow.
- **MIDDLE (1600 px):** depth now works — figures shrink from the foreground (h ≈ 300 px) to the
  troops at the wall (h ≈ 50 px), and the elephants, carpet, fountain and terrace all sit at
  different distances. The three actions are findable: audience at the terrace, musicians at the
  right carpet, servants at the left with a dropped tray. **PASS.**
- **NEAR (100%):** the figure construction resolves into turban, jama, sash and arms; the drone-birds
  show their rotors and body; the parterre's flowers are drawn. **Weak point:** the elephants read as
  grey blobs with a cabinet on top — the howdah floated above the back and the head was a circle. Fixed
  in pass 4 (ears, head, trunk, howdah seated on the back).

**Where the eye goes:** first to the white pavilion (brightest), then down the central channel to the
fountain, then to the elephants, then — if it is patient — to the one figure in the empty quadrant.
That is the intended order: the rule of the garden is the last thing you see.
## Critique

- **What works:** the plan reads first - a chahar bagh with four quadrants and a cross of water - and
  the crowd rule is visible: three quadrants hold the durbar, the fourth holds one kneeling gardener,
  so the picture can be checked by counting. Depth is carried by one scale function (figure height
  rises with the ground line) and by four planes: cropped foreground figures, near garden, far garden,
  wall and trees. The three actions are all findable without a caption.
- **What is weak:** the pavilion is a gazebo, not a jharokha: dome, pillars and cornice are right in
  parts but the platform reads as a white band, and at 400 px the terrace figures are specks. The
  parterre is a regular dot grid, which reads as measles rather than planted beds. The drone-birds
  are legible as birds but not as drones until 100%; I would make three of them deliberately wrong
  (rotors too big) so the find-it starts at middle distance.
- **Where the eye goes:** pavilion (brightest), then the channel, the fountain, the elephants, and
  last the lone gardener. That is the intended route: the rule of the garden is the last thing seen.
