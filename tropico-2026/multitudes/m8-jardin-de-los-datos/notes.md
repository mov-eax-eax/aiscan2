# El Jardín de los Datos — notes

**Idiom:** Boschian garden. **Canvas:** 1600 x 1200 landscape.

## Argument (before drawing)

What the garden knows that the caption will not say: the beds are a catalogue, not a landscape. Each
bed grows one species of device, and the gardeners tend them the way monks tend relics - watering a
rack, pruning a cable, feeding a router. The procession carries screens because screens are what this
garden harvests. Nine small naked people are loose in it, doing absurd things, exactly as in a Bosch:
the absurdity is the only sane response to a garden that has replaced its animals with instruments.

## Crowd rule

The garden is six beds in a 3 x 2 grid; every bed holds exactly one device species (rack hedge,
root machines, cable fountain, monitor pavilion, router tree, antenna animals). Every human belongs
to a bed and is doing that bed a service - except the procession, the only group allowed to cross
beds. Secondary rule: every third gardener carries a tool.

## Find-it motif

A small naked human: 9 occurrences - sitting astride a falling cable, inside a rack, riding a router
in the tree, carrying a screen on the head, bathing in the cable fountain, asleep in a bed, feeding an
antenna-animal, measuring a machine with outstretched arms, hanging from the pavilion beam.

## Three simultaneous actions

1. The watering (left-front bed): a gardener waters a server rack that is sprouting leaves.
2. The tug (centre, foreground): two men pull opposite ends of one cable, a third is about to cut it.
3. The procession (crossing the beds): ten figures carry screens on poles toward the pavilion.

## Pass log (3 passes)

1. Build: sky, distant hills, six beds, the device species, the procession, the find-it figures.
   First render read as a diagram of six rooms: bare beds, floating screens, timid naked figures.
2. Beds given soil furrows, hedge borders and planted dots; the sky filled with a distant city and a
   tree line; the procession screens lowered onto their poles; the naked figures enlarged.

3. Final: value washes, shadows under the pavilion and the fountain, grain. CHECKPOINT BELOW.

## Checkpoint — pass 3

- **FAR (400 px):** a pale garden divided into six beds, each with a different machine, crowds of
  small figures, and a pale horizon. It reads as a garden of devices, though the bed borders make it
  read as six panels rather than one garden.

- **MIDDLE (1600 px):** the six species are distinguishable (racks, root machines, the cable
  fountain, the monitor pavilion, the router tree, the antenna animals), the procession crosses the
  middle, and the tug reads in the foreground. The three actions are findable.

- **NEAR (100%):** the rack rows with their blinking lights and leaves, the monitors, the routers,
  the nine naked figures and their absurd acts.

**Where the eye goes:** the cable fountain, then the monitor pavilion, then the procession.

## Critique

- **What works:** the catalogue logic is visible - each bed is a species, each device is tended, and
  the procession is the one thing that crosses the grid, so the picture is a garden and an inventory
  at once. The naked figures are the right kind of absurdity and they are findable.

- **What is weak:** the six beds read as six framed panels, which flattens the garden into a diagram;
  the horizon city is a row of grey boxes; and the figures are all at one scale, so the four depth
  planes are asserted by the layout rather than carried by scale. The Bosch quality - teeming,
  uncomfortable, detailed - is only partly there: the scene is legible but not dense enough at 100%.

- **What I would change:** remove the bed frames and let the beds bleed into paths (depth by
  overlap, not by frames), scale the foreground figures up by 2x and crop them at the edge, and put
  a third of the detail budget into the foreground instead of spreading it evenly.
